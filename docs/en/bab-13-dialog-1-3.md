# Chapter 13 · Dialogues 1–3

## Dialogue 1: Job interview

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 请坐。你以前做过什么工作？</div>
<div class="py">Qǐng zuò. Nǐ yǐqián zuò guo shénme gōngzuò?</div>
<div>Please sit down. What work have you done before?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 我以前在商店工作过。我卖过衣服，也卖过鞋。</div>
<div class="py">Wǒ yǐqián zài shāngdiàn gōngzuò guo. Wǒ mài guo yīfu, yě mài guo xié.</div>
<div>I worked at a store before. I sold clothes, and shoes too.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 你有几年的工作经验？</div>
<div class="py">Nǐ yǒu jǐ nián de gōngzuò jīngyàn?</div>
<div>How many years of work experience do you have?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 三年。我的能力不错，学东西很快。</div>
<div class="py">Sān nián. Wǒ de nénglì bùcuò, xué dōngxi hěn kuài.</div>
<div>Three years. My skills are solid, I learn things fast.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 好极了！你为什么想来我们公司？</div>
<div class="py">Hǎo jí le! Nǐ wèishénme xiǎng lái wǒmen gōngsī?</div>
<div>Excellent! Why do you want to join our company?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 因为你们公司很有名，我一直想来这里工作。</div>
<div class="py">Yīnwèi nǐmen gōngsī hěn yǒumíng, wǒ yìzhí xiǎng lái zhèlǐ gōngzuò.</div>
<div>Because your company is famous, I've always wanted to work here.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 你什么时候能开始工作？</div>
<div class="py">Nǐ shénme shíhou néng kāishǐ gōngzuò?</div>
<div>When can you start work?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 下个星期一就可以。谢谢给我这个机会！</div>
<div class="py">Xià ge xīngqī yī jiù kěyǐ. Xièxie gěi wǒ zhè ge jīhuì!</div>
<div>Next Monday works. Thanks for giving me this opportunity!</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 不客气，欢迎你加入我们！我们下个星期给你打电话。</div>
<div class="py">Bú kèqi, huānyíng nǐ jiārù wǒmen! Wǒmen xià ge xīngqī gěi nǐ dǎ diànhuà.</div>
<div>You're welcome, welcome aboard! We'll call you next week.</div>
</div>

**Dialogue breakdown:**

- **面试** *miànshì* ("job interview"): the keyword of this dialogue. 试 = to try.
- **经验** *jīngyàn* ("experience"): 有经验 = experienced, 没经验 = no experience yet.
- **能力** *nénglì* ("ability"): 能力不错 = solid skills.
- **请坐** *qǐng zuò* ("please sit"): 请 + V = polite request (light causative, Chapter 12 pattern).
- **好极了** *hǎo jí le* ("excellent"): Adj + 极了 = casual superlative (Chapter 12 pattern).
- **一直** *yìzhí* ("all along"): 一直想 = have always wanted.
- **机会** *jīhuì* ("opportunity"): 给机会 = give a chance.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why this pattern?</div>
<p>A Chinese job interview is <strong>direct but polite</strong>. The interviewer asks straight about experience (经验) and ability (能力), and the candidate answers with concrete numbers ("three years"). At the end, the interviewer never says "you're hired" on the spot, instead "we'll call you next week". That's polite code: no call means no offer. Don't just wait around; smart candidates keep applying elsewhere.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 请 + V for polite invitations also appears in Dialogue 5 (making plans by phone).</p>
</div>

## Dialogue 2: Office meeting

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 会议开始。昨天的计划被老板改了。</div>
<div class="py">Huìyì kāishǐ. Zuótiān de jìhuà bèi lǎobǎn gǎi le.</div>
<div>Meeting starts. Yesterday's plan was changed by the boss.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 为什么被改了？我们讨论了很久。</div>
<div class="py">Wèishénme bèi gǎi le? Wǒmen tǎolùn le hěn jiǔ.</div>
<div>Why was it changed? We discussed it for ages.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 老板说钱不多，我们必须少花一点儿。</div>
<div class="py">Lǎobǎn shuō qián bù duō, wǒmen bìxū shǎo huā yìdiǎnr.</div>
<div>The boss says money is tight, we must spend a bit less.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 除了钱的问题，我们还有别的办法吗？</div>
<div class="py">Chúle qián de wèntǐ, wǒmen hái yǒu bié de bànfǎ ma?</div>
<div>Besides the money issue, do we have other options?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 我们决定先不买新电脑，把旧的修好再说。</div>
<div class="py">Wǒmen juédìng xiān bù mǎi xīn diànnǎo, bǎ jiù de xiū hǎo zàishuō.</div>
<div>We decided not to buy new computers for now, fix the old ones first then talk.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 好，我同意。什么时候开始修？</div>
<div class="py">Hǎo, wǒ tóngyì. Shénme shíhou kāishǐ xiū?</div>
<div>OK, I agree. When do we start fixing?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 明天就开始，请大家一起帮忙。</div>
<div class="py">Míngtiān jiù kāishǐ, qǐng dàjiā yìqǐ bāngmáng.</div>
<div>Starting tomorrow, please everyone pitch in.</div>
</div>

**Dialogue breakdown:**

- **会议** *huìyì* ("meeting"): 会 = gathering, 议 = discuss.
- **被** *bèi* (passive marker): 被老板改了 = was changed by the boss (Chapter 12 pattern).
- **计划** *jìhuà* ("plan"): 做计划 = make a plan.
- **必须** *bìxū* ("must"): stronger than 应该, non-negotiable.
- **除了…以外** *chúle…yǐwài* ("besides…"): 除了钱的问题 = besides the money issue (Chapter 12 pattern).
- **决定** *juédìng* ("to decide"): 决定 + V = decide to….
- **把旧的修好** *bǎ jiù de xiū hǎo* ("fix the old ones"): 把 + object + V + result complement (full Chapter 12 pattern).

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why this pattern?</div>
<p>In Chinese office meetings, the passive with <strong>被</strong> delivers bad news without pointing fingers: "the plan was changed by the boss" (被老板改了) sounds softer than "the boss changed the plan". It's not dodging, it's saving face (面子) for everyone. Also note: decisions are framed collectively ("we decided"), not as one person's order, the standard style in Chinese companies.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> the 被 passive also fits complaints in Dialogue 4 (shopping).</p>
</div>

## Dialogue 3: Asking for directions

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 请问，地铁站在哪儿？</div>
<div class="py">Qǐngwèn, dìtiě zhàn zài nǎr?</div>
<div>Excuse me, where is the subway station?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 你往前走，走到红绿灯，再往左转。</div>
<div class="py">Nǐ wǎng qián zǒu, zǒu dào hónglǜdēng, zài wǎng zuǒ zhuǎn.</div>
<div>Walk straight ahead, go to the traffic light, then turn left.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 转过去以后呢？</div>
<div class="py">Zhuǎn guoqu yǐhòu ne?</div>
<div>And after turning?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 转过去以后往下走，地铁站就在下面。</div>
<div class="py">Zhuǎn guoqu yǐhòu wǎng xià zǒu, dìtiě zhàn jiù zài xiàmian.</div>
<div>After turning, go down: the station is right below.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 远吗？要走多长时间？</div>
<div class="py">Yuǎn ma? Yào zǒu duō cháng shíjiān?</div>
<div>Is it far? How long is the walk?</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">B: 不远，走五分钟就到了。你一边走一边看地图吧。</div>
<div class="py">Bù yuǎn, zǒu wǔ fēnzhōng jiù dào le. Nǐ yìbiān zǒu yìbiān kàn dìtú ba.</div>
<div>Not far, five minutes on foot. Just walk while checking the map.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh">
<div class="hz">A: 谢谢你！</div>
<div class="py">Xièxie nǐ!</div>
<div>Thank you!</div>
</div>

**Dialogue breakdown:**

- **地铁** *dìtiě* ("subway"): 铁 = iron, the "iron dragon" underground.
- **往前走** *wǎng qián zǒu* ("walk ahead"): 往 + direction + V (Chapter 8 pattern, reused).
- **红绿灯** *hónglǜdēng* ("traffic light"): 红 + 绿 + 灯, the easiest landmark for directions.
- **转过去** *zhuǎn guoqu* ("turn over there"): V + 过去 = directional complement away from speaker (Chapter 12 pattern).
- **往下走** *wǎng xià zǒu* ("go down"): subway stations are usually underground.
- **一边…一边…** *yìbiān…yìbiān…* ("while…"): two actions at once (Chapter 12 pattern).
- **地图** *dìtú* ("map"): 图 = picture.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why this pattern?</div>
<p>Chinese people give directions with <strong>landmarks and directional complements</strong>, not "turn at the minimart". The pattern is always: 往 + direction (往前走), then V + 过去/过来 for "over there / over here". If you only memorized left-right without directional complements, you'll get lost in real conversations. Drill this dialogue until 转过去 comes out of your mouth automatically, not just 转.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> directional complements also appear in Dialogue 4 (拿回来, 穿上).</p>
</div>
