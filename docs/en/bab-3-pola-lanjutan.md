# Chapter 3 · 3.5–3.7: Advanced Patterns

## 3.5 Three bonus patterns you'll see a lot

Not classical radicals, but components that repeat in HSK 1 and are immediately useful:

**电 (diàn, electricity)**, everything electric:
<div class="bedah"><strong>电</strong> (diàn) = 日 + 乚 → "lightning" → electricity. Used in <strong>电话</strong> (diànhuà, telephone), <strong>电脑</strong> (diànnǎo, computer), <strong>电视</strong> (diànshì, television), <strong>电影</strong> (diànyǐng, movie).</div>

<!-- KENAPA:dian -->
<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 电话/电脑/电视/电影 all start with 电?</div>
<p><strong>电</strong> (diàn) = 日 + 乚, legend has it it pictures <em>lightning</em> striking, then the meaning broadened to "electricity". Every modern electric-powered technology uses 电 as its "family name": <strong>电话</strong> (diànhuà, telephone), <strong>电脑</strong> (diànnǎo, computer), <strong>电视</strong> (diànshì, television), <strong>电影</strong> (diànyǐng, movie).</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The "one component = one tech family" pattern, like the 子 family for things. Once you memorize 电 = electricity, those four words stick together as a pack.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> 电影 (movie) does use 电, but it means "moving shadows", not literally "electricity", word families give hints, not definitions.</p>
</div>

**艹 (cǎo, grass/plant)**, everything that grows:
<div class="bedah"><strong>菜</strong> (cài, vegetables) = 艹 (plant) + 采; <strong>苹</strong> (píng, in <strong>苹果</strong> píngguǒ, apple) = 艹 + 平; <strong>茶</strong> (chá, tea) uses it too.</div>

<!-- KENAPA:ge -->
<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 我 (I/me) and 钱 (money) look alike?</div>
<p>Both carry the component <strong>戈</strong> (gē, ancient spear/battle-axe). <strong>我</strong> (wǒ) = 手 (hand) + 戈, legend has it it pictures "a hand holding a spear", then the meaning broadened from "weapon (of mine)" to "I/me". Simplified <strong>钱</strong> = 钅 (metal) + 戋, and 戋 is supposedly <em>two stacked 戈</em>, ancient coins were indeed minted to look like tools.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The "same component shows up in unexpected words" pattern: once you know 戈, it becomes a visual anchor for recognizing both 我 and 钱.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> This is a common etymology theory, not absolute certainty, and the shape similarity doesn't mean the meanings are related ("I" vs "money" clearly have nothing to do with each other).</p>
</div>

**贝 (bèi, cowrie shell)**, shells were ancient currency, so it's about money:
<div class="bedah"><strong>买</strong> (mǎi, to buy) puts 贝 at the bottom → an activity involving "money".</div>

<!-- KENAPA:bei -->
<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 买 and 钱 both use 贝 (cowrie shell)?</div>
<p>Before coins existed, ancient Chinese society used <strong>shells as currency</strong>. The trace stuck to the characters: anything that "smells of money" tends to carry 贝. <strong>买</strong> (mǎi, to buy) puts 贝 at the bottom; <strong>钱</strong> (qián, money) = 钅 (metal) + 戋, and 戋 itself is supposedly two stacked <strong>戈</strong> (spears), but the simplified version still belongs to the "wealth" meaning family.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The "贝 = money" pattern is very productive (outside HSK 1): <strong>贵</strong> (guì, expensive), <strong>费</strong> (fèi, fee), <strong>货</strong> (huò, merchandise). See 贝, think money first.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> Not everything with 贝 is about money, but in HSK 1, both 买 and 钱 are.</p>
</div>

## 3.6 Basic stroke order

Characters are written in a standard stroke order (笔顺, bǐshùn). No need to be perfect, but following the pattern keeps your writing neat and easier to remember. The eight basic rules:

1. **Top → bottom** (从上到下): 三 (sān), write the topmost line first.
2. **Left → right** (从左到右): 你 (nǐ), 吃 (chī), left component first.
3. **Horizontal → vertical** (先横后竖): 十 (shí), 木 (mù), the 一 stroke first, then 丨.
4. **撇 stroke → 捺 stroke** (先撇后捺): 人 (rén), 大 (dà), 丿 first, then ㇏.
5. **Outside → inside** (先外后内): 同 (tóng, in 同学), outer frame first, then the inside.
6. **Full box: outside → inside → close** (先外后内再封口): 国 (guó, in 中国), left-top-right sides, then the inside (玉), bottom line last.
7. **Middle → sides** (先中间后两边): 小 (xiǎo), 水 (shuǐ), middle stroke first, then left-right.
8. **Top-right dot last**: 我 (wǒ), 太 (tài), the 丶 dot is written last.

Writing practice: pick one character from each radical in section 3.4, write it 5 times while naming its components. Writing + naming components sticks way better than writing blindly.

## 3.7 Serial patterns: HSK 1 word families

Radicals group characters by meaning. But there's another pattern that's just as useful: **words that share one common component**, like a family. Memorize one member, and the rest tag along.

### The 子 family, all "things"

Concrete nouns ending in 子:

| Word | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 桌子 | zhuōzi | table |
| 椅子 | yǐzi | chair |
| 杯子 | bēizi | glass |
| 儿子 | érzi | son |

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 桌子/椅子/杯子 all end with 子?</div>
<p>In modern Mandarin, many single-character nouns get "lengthened" with <strong>子</strong> so they sound like full, concrete nouns: 桌 + 子, 椅 + 子, 杯 + 子. Legend has it this is a sound legacy, a single syllable feels "incomplete" as a standalone word, so 子 gets attached.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The same pattern in HSK 1: <strong>儿子</strong> (érzi, son) = 儿 + 子, <strong>名字</strong> (míngzi, name). All concrete things/people.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> But <strong>女儿</strong> (nǚ'ér, daughter) uses <strong>儿</strong>, not 子, and abstract words don't use this pattern. Don't stick 子 onto random words.</p>
</div>
<div class="py">zhuōzi shàng yǒu bēizi.</div>
<div>There's a glass on the table.</div>
</div>

### The 学 family, all about "learning"

| Word | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 学校 | xuéxiào | school |
| 学生 | xuéshēng | student |
| 学习 | xuéxí | to study |

<div class="bedah"><strong>学</strong> (xué) = ⺍ + 冖 (roof) + 子 (child) → "a child under a roof" = to learn. Three HSK 1 words use it: 学校 (place of learning), 学生 (person who learns), 学习 (the activity of learning).</div>

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 学校/学生/学习 share 学, and why is 学 itself = a child under a roof?</div>
<p><strong>学</strong> (xué) = ⺍ + 冖 (roof) + 子 (child), legend has it it pictures "a child under a roof" (a place of learning). From this one root come three roles: <strong>学校</strong> (xuéxiào) = the place of learning, <strong>学生</strong> (xuéshēng) = the person who learns (生 = born/emerge → "one born of learning"), <strong>学习</strong> (xuéxí) = the activity of learning (习 = to practice).</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The "one root, three roles (place–person–activity)" pattern, once you memorize 学, these three HSK 1 words become one package, not three separate memorizations.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> Don't flip it: not every word with 学 literally means "learning", here all three just happen to be one meaning family.</p>
</div>
<div class="py">xuéshēng zài xuéxiào xuéxí Hànyǔ.</div>
<div>Students study Mandarin at school.</div>
</div>

### The 果 family, all "fruit"

| Word | Pinyin | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 水果 | shuǐguǒ | fruit |
| 苹果 | píngguǒ | apple |

<div class="bedah"><strong>果</strong> (guǒ, fruit) = 田 (field) + 木 (tree) → "a tree's yield in the field" = fruit. <strong>苹果</strong> (píngguǒ) = 苹 + 果 → apple.</div>

::: tip
Memorization technique: every time you meet a new word, ask "which family does it belong to?", 子? 电? 学? Once you find its family, you've half-guessed the meaning before opening a dictionary.
:::
