# Chapter 3 · 3.1–3.4: Core Radicals

## 3.1 What is a radical?

A **radical** (部首, bùshǒu, literally "section header") is the character component used as a dictionary category. Think of it like the first letter in an English dictionary: all words starting with "m" get grouped in one section.

Quick facts:

- The classic **Kangxi** dictionary established **214 radicals**.
- Simplified Chinese (used in China, and what we're learning) uses about **201** of them.
- One radical can change shape depending on its **position** inside a character:

| Full radical | Variant form | Position | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 人 rén (person) | 亻 | left | 你, 他 |
| 心 xīn (heart) | 忄 | left | 想 |
| 手 shǒu (hand) | 扌 | left | 打 |
| 水 shuǐ (water) | 氵 | left | 汉 |
| 言 yán (speech) | 讠 | left | 说 |
| 食 shí (food) | 饣 | left | 饭 |

Radicals usually give a **meaning hint** (not an exact meaning). Example: characters with the 口 (mouth) radical almost always relate to the mouth, sound, or speech, for example 叫 (to call), 吃 (to eat), 喝 (to drink).

## 3.2 The "guess the meaning from components" technique

More than 80% of characters are **形声字** (xíngshēngzì, "sound-form characters"): one component hints at the **meaning**, another hints at the **sound**. The breakdown technique has four steps:

1. **Find the radical**, usually on the left, top, or bottom of the character.
2. **Guess the meaning category** from that radical (mouth? water? hand? woman?).
3. **Look at the remaining component**, it's often a sound hint, not a meaning hint.
4. **Check a dictionary** to confirm. A guess is still a guess.

Walkthrough:

<div class="bedah"><strong>妈</strong> (mā, in <strong>妈妈</strong> māma, mother) = 女 (woman → meaning hint) + 马 (mǎ → sound hint, read almost like <em>mā</em>). So: "a woman who sounds like <em>ma</em>" = mother.</div>

This technique is what lets you guess the meaning of unfamiliar characters, exactly like what you did when you broke down 位 = 亻 + 立 and 拉 = 扌 + 立: same right-side component, the left radical decides the meaning.

## 3.3 Its limits: not always accurate

The breakdown technique is a helper, not a math formula. Four limits:

1. **The logic is sometimes ancient cultural logic, not universal logic.** 好 (good) = 女 (woman) + 子 (child). "Woman + child = good" makes sense in ancient Chinese culture, but you can't derive it purely from logic.
2. **Sound hints often miss.** 吗 (ma) uses 马 (mǎ) as a sound hint, the sounds are close, the tones differ. Sometimes it misses by a mile.
3. **Simplification breaks the hints.** 爱 (to love) in traditional form 愛 still has 心 (heart) at the bottom; the simplified version lost its heart component.
4. **Meanings can drift into the idiomatic.** 东西 (thing) = 东 (east) + 西 (west). From the components, there's no way to guess it means "thing".

The rule: **break it down to guess, use a dictionary to confirm.**

## 3.4 The fifteen most important HSK 1 radicals

### 1. 人 / 亻, rén, "person"

The most human of radicals: characters with it relate to people or human activities.

- 你 nǐ, you
- 他 tā, he
- 我们 wǒmen, we/us
- 什么 shénme, what
- 住 zhù, to live (somewhere)
- 做 zuò, to do; to make
- 时候 shíhou, time; moment

<div class="bedah"><strong>你</strong> (nǐ, you) = 亻 (person) + 尔 (sound hint) → second-person pronoun.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>他</strong> (tā, he) = 亻 (person) + 也 (sound hint) → third-person pronoun.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>们</strong> (men, in <strong>我们</strong> wǒmen, we/us) = 亻 (person) + 门 (mén, sound hint) → plural marker reserved for humans.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>什</strong> (shén, in <strong>什么</strong> shénme, what) = 亻 (person) + 十 (ten). The components give no clear meaning hint: 什么 is a question word to memorize whole; don't force "person + ten = what".</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>住</strong> (zhù, to live) = 亻 (person) + 主 (zhǔ, sound hint) → to live/settle.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>做</strong> (zuò, to do/make) = 亻 (person) + 故 (gù, sound hint) → to do/make.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>候</strong> (hòu, in <strong>时候</strong> shíhou, time) = 亻 (person) + 侯 (hóu, sound hint). The meaning "moment" comes from usage of 时候, not from the components.</div>

### 2. 口, kǒu, "mouth"

Related to the mouth, sound, and speech.

- 叫 jiào, to call; to be named
- 吃 chī, to eat
- 喝 hē, to drink
- 吗 ma, question particle
- 哪 nǎ, which
- 呢 ne, follow-up question particle
- 听 tīng, to hear

<div class="bedah"><strong>叫</strong> (jiào, to call) = 口 (mouth) + 丩 → an activity using the mouth/voice.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>吃</strong> (chī, to eat) = 口 (mouth) + 乞 → something that goes in through the mouth.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>喝</strong> (hē, to drink) = 口 (mouth) + 曷 → something that goes in through the mouth.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>吗</strong> (ma, question particle) = 口 (mouth/speech) + 马 (mǎ, sound hint) → a particle spoken at the end of a question.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>哪</strong> (nǎ, which) = 口 + 那 (nà, sound hint). A question word to memorize whole; the components only hint at the sound.</div>

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why do 妈 and 吗 share the same right-side component (马)?</div>
<p>Both use <strong>马</strong> (mǎ, horse) as a <em>sound hint</em>: 妈 is read mā, 吗 is read ma, close to the sound of 马. What decides the <em>meaning and function</em> is the left radical: <strong>女</strong> (woman) → 妈 = mother; <strong>口</strong> (mouth/speech) → 吗 = the question particle you say out loud.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> This is the common 形声 (form + sound) character pattern: the right/bottom component gives the sound hint, the radical gives the meaning hint. Another HSK 1 example: <strong>她</strong> (tā) = 女 + 也, 也 is the same sound hint used by <strong>他</strong> (tā).</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> Sound hints often miss the tone (mǎ → mā/ma) and sometimes miss by a lot, they're only "roughly", never a guarantee.</p>
</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>呢</strong> (ne, follow-up question particle) = 口 (mouth/speech) + 尼 (sound hint) → a particle spoken at the end of a sentence, e.g. 你呢？ (what about you?).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>听</strong> (tīng, to hear) = 口 + 斤. Careful: this is a simplification trap, the traditional version <strong>聽</strong> uses 耳 (ear), which makes way more sense. The simplified version borrows 口 + 斤 as a compact form, so don't swallow its "mouth" breakdown whole.</div>

### 3. 女, nǚ, "woman"

Related to women, family, and traits.

- 好 hǎo, good; nice
- 妈妈 māma, mother
- 小姐 xiǎojiě, miss; young lady
- 女儿 nǚ'ér, daughter
- 她 tā, she

<div class="bedah"><strong>好</strong> (hǎo, good) = 女 (woman) + 子 (child) → "woman + child = good". An example of the breakdown technique's limits: the logic is cultural, not universal.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>妈</strong> (mā, in <strong>妈妈</strong> māma, mother) = 女 (woman) + 马 (mǎ, sound hint) → mother.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>姐</strong> (jiě, in <strong>小姐</strong> xiǎojiě, miss) = 女 (woman) + 且 (sound hint) → elder sister → "miss" (polite address).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>女儿</strong> (nǚ'ér, daughter) = 女 (woman) + 儿 (child) → daughter. (Here 儿 reads ér; don't confuse it with the 子 suffix in 桌子.)</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>她</strong> (tā, she) = 女 (woman) + 也 (sound hint, same as 他) → the female counterpart of 他. Don't mix up: 他 he, 她 she, read exactly the same.</div>

<!-- KENAPA:hao -->
<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is 好 (good) = 女 + 子?</div>
<p>Legend has it, in ancient China's agrarian society, the sight of "a woman with a child" was seen as the picture of goodness and good fortune, a whole, thriving family. So 好 isn't a random combo: it stores its era's cultural view of what "good" is.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The other 女 family members in HSK 1: <strong>妈</strong> (mā, mother) = 女 + 马, <strong>她</strong> (tā, she) = 女 + 也. All of them "smell of" women/family.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> This logic is cultural, not universal, exactly as discussed in section 3.3. Don't force the "woman = good" logic onto other modern characters outside this list.</p>
</div>

### 4. 忄 / 心, xīn, "heart"

Related to feelings, thoughts, and desires. The 忄 form is used on the left, 心 at the bottom.

- 想 xiǎng, to want; to think
- 怎么 zěnme, how

<div class="bedah"><strong>想</strong> (xiǎng, to want/think) = 相 + 心 (heart) → something "in the heart" = to think about / to want.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>怎</strong> (zěn, in <strong>怎么</strong> zěnme, how) = 乍 + 心. The question word "how" is memorized whole; the components give no clear meaning hint.</div>

<!-- KENAPA:xiang -->
<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is 想 (to want / to think) = 相 + 心?</div>
<p><strong>相</strong> (xiāng) on its own means "mutual/visible", and <strong>心</strong> is the heart. Something "visible in the heart" = what's thought and wanted. Ancient Chinese put thoughts and feelings in one place: the heart, not the brain.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The "heart = feelings/thoughts" pattern repeats in other characters (outside HSK 1): <strong>念</strong> (niàn, to miss someone) = 今 + 心, <strong>思</strong> (sī, to think) = 田 + 心. In HSK 1, <strong>怎么</strong> (zěnme, how) also uses 心, even asking "how" involves the heart.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> <strong>相</strong> here isn't literally "mutual", it works more as a sound hint (xiāng → xiǎng, different tone). Don't read 想 as "mutual hearts".</p>
</div>

In HSK 1 this radical is genuinely rare, but it matters: almost every character about feelings uses it.

### 5. 扌, shǒu, "hand"

The variant form of 手 on the left. Related to hand-based activities.

- 打 dǎ, (in 打电话) to make a phone call
- 打电话 dǎ diànhuà, to make a phone call

<div class="bedah"><strong>打</strong> (dǎ) = 扌 (hand) + 丁 → originally "to hit". <strong>打电话</strong> (dǎ diànhuà) literally "to hit the telephone", a leftover from rotary phones you dialed by hand, means to make a phone call.</div>

In HSK 1 there's only one word with this radical, but the pattern is crystal clear: see 扌, think "hand".

### 6. 氵, shuǐ, "water"

The variant form of 水 on the left. Related to water and liquids.

- 水 shuǐ, water
- 汉语 Hànyǔ, Mandarin
- 没 méi, not (past/not yet)

<div class="bedah"><strong>水</strong> (shuǐ, water), the radical is itself a whole character: three drops of water.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>汉</strong> (Hàn, in <strong>汉语</strong> Hànyǔ) = 氵 (water) + 又 → named after the Han River → the Han people → the Han people's language = Mandarin.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>没</strong> (méi, not) = 氵 + 殳. Here 氵 is not a "water" meaning hint, it's just an inherited shape component. An example that radicals don't always give a clear meaning hint; sometimes they just "stick around" historically.</div>

### 7. 木, mù, "wood"

Related to wood, trees, and wooden objects.

- 本 běn, measure word for books
- 茶 chá, tea
- 椅子 yǐzi, chair
- 飞机 fēijī, airplane
- 学校 xuéxiào, school
- 桌子 zhuōzi, table

<div class="bedah"><strong>本</strong> (běn) = 木 (wood/tree) + 一 (line at the root) → "tree root" → used as the measure word for books (books were once written on bamboo/wooden strips).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>茶</strong> (chá, tea) = 艹 (grass/plant) + 人 + 木 → a woody plant whose leaves get brewed.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>椅</strong> (yǐ, in <strong>椅子</strong> yǐzi, chair) = 木 (wood) + 奇 → chair, which used to always be wooden.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>机</strong> (jī, in <strong>飞机</strong> fēijī, airplane) = 木 (wood) + 几 → originally "machine/device" (ancient machines were wooden) → airplane.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>校</strong> (xiào, in <strong>学校</strong> xuéxiào, school) = 木 (wood) + 交 (jiāo, sound hint). The meaning "school" comes from usage of 学校, not from the components.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>桌</strong> (zhuō, in <strong>桌子</strong> zhuōzi, table) = 卓 + 木 (wood) → table (always wooden back then).</div>

### 8. 日, rì, "sun"

Related to the sun, light, the sky, and time.

- 明天 míngtiān, tomorrow
- 时候 shíhou, time; moment
- 星期 xīngqī, week
- 昨天 zuótiān, yesterday

<div class="bedah"><strong>明</strong> (míng, in <strong>明天</strong> míngtiān, tomorrow) = 日 (sun) + 月 (moon) → "bright" → the bright day = tomorrow.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>时</strong> (shí, in <strong>时候</strong> shíhou, time) = 日 (sun) + 寸 → time (in olden days time was read from the sun's shadow).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>星</strong> (xīng, in <strong>星期</strong> xīngqī, week) = 日 (celestial body) + 生 → star.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>昨</strong> (zuó, in <strong>昨天</strong> zuótiān, yesterday) = 日 (sun/day) + 乍 → "the day (that's passed)" = yesterday. Paired with 明天: 明 = the bright day (tomorrow), 昨 = the dark/past day (yesterday).</div>

### 9. 月, yuè, "moon" / "meat"

A double-duty radical: it can mean moon, but more often it's the variant form of **肉** (ròu, meat) meaning **body**.

- 有 yǒu, to have; there is
- 朋友 péngyou, friend
- 衣服 yīfu, clothes
- 前面 qiánmian, front
- 星期 xīngqī, week

<div class="bedah"><strong>有</strong> (yǒu, to have) = 𠂇 (hand) + 月 (meat) → "a hand holding meat" = to possess.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>朋</strong> (péng, in <strong>朋友</strong> péngyou, friend) = 月 + 月 → "two pieces of meat side by side" = companion.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>服</strong> (fú, in <strong>衣服</strong> yīfu, clothes) uses 月 (meat/body) → something that covers the body.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>前</strong> (qián, in <strong>前面</strong> qiánmian, front): the 月 component here gives no clear meaning hint; "front" is memorized whole.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>期</strong> (qī, in <strong>星期</strong> xīngqī, week) = 其 + 月 → "period of time". 星期 = "period of (counting) stars" = week (seven days).</div>

### 10. 讠 / 言, yán, "speech"

The variant form of 言 on the left. Related to speaking and language.

- 说话 shuōhuà, to speak
- 认识 rènshi, to know (someone); to recognize
- 汉语 Hànyǔ, Mandarin
- 电话 diànhuà, telephone
- 谁 shéi, who
- 读 dú, to read

<div class="bedah"><strong>说</strong> (shuō, in <strong>说话</strong> shuōhuà, to speak) = 讠 (speech) + 兑 → to speak.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>话</strong> (huà, in <strong>说话</strong> and <strong>电话</strong>) = 讠 (speech) + 舌 (tongue) → words. So <strong>电话</strong> (diànhuà) = 电 (electricity) + 话 (speech) → "speech via electricity" = telephone.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>认</strong> (rèn, in <strong>认识</strong> rènshi, to recognize) = 讠 (speech) + 人 → to recognize (someone through introduction/words).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>识</strong> (shi, in <strong>认识</strong> rènshi, to recognize) = 讠 (speech) + 只 → "to mark through words" = to recognize. So 认识 = 认 + 识, both speech-radical characters, recognizing someone really does happen through introductions and conversation.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>语</strong> (yǔ, in <strong>汉语</strong> Hànyǔ, Mandarin) = 讠 (speech) + 吾 → language.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>谁</strong> (shéi, who) = 讠 (speech) + 隹 (sound hint). A question word for people, memorized whole.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>读</strong> (dú, to read) = 讠 (speech) + 卖 → to read (in olden days reading meant reading aloud, hence the speech radical).</div>

### 11. 大, dà, "big"

- 大 dà, big
- 太 tài, too
- 天气 tiānqì, weather
- 今天 jīntiān, today

<div class="bedah"><strong>大</strong> (dà, big) = 人 (person) + 一 → "a person spreading their arms" = big.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>太</strong> (tài, too) = 大 (big) + 丶 (dot) → "more than big" = too.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>天</strong> (tiān, in <strong>天气</strong> tiānqì and <strong>今天</strong> jīntiān) = 大 + 一 → "what's above humans" = sky → day/weather.</div>

### 12. 子, zǐ, "child"

In HSK 1, 子 most often appears as a **suffix marking small/concrete things**, a very productive pattern.

- 桌子 zhuōzi, table
- 杯子 bēizi, cup; glass
- 名字 míngzi, name
- 儿子 érzi, son
- 椅子 yǐzi, chair

<div class="bedah"><strong>桌子</strong> (zhuōzi, table) = 桌 + 子 (thing suffix) → the thing is a table.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>杯子</strong> (bēizi, glass) = 杯 (木 + 不) + 子 (thing suffix) → the thing is a glass.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>字</strong> (zì, in <strong>名字</strong> míngzi, name) = 宀 (roof) + 子 (child) → character. Meanwhile <strong>名</strong> (míng, name) = 夕 (dusk) + 口 (mouth): legend says in the dark people are recognized by voice, not face, so a "name" is what the mouth calls out. The exact origin is debated; just memorize 名字 = name.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>儿子</strong> (érzi, son) = 儿 (child) + 子 → child.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>椅子</strong> (yǐzi, chair) = 椅 (木 + 奇) + 子 (thing suffix) → the thing is a chair.</div>

Careful note: 女儿 (nǚ'ér, daughter) really does end with 儿, not 子, don't mix up the patterns. The 子 suffix pattern is purely for things: 桌子, 椅子, 杯子.

### 13. 宀, mián, "roof"

Related to houses, buildings, and dwellings.

- 家 jiā, home; family
- 字 zì, character

<div class="bedah"><strong>家</strong> (jiā, home/family) = 宀 (roof) + 豕 (pig) → "a pig under a roof" = home. Makes sense in ancient agrarian society: a home was where humans and livestock sheltered.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>字</strong> (zì, character) = 宀 (roof) + 子 (child). The exact origin is debated. What matters: 字 always means character, as in 名字 (name) and 汉字 (Hànzì, Chinese characters).</div>

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is 家 (home/family) = 宀 (roof) + 豕 (pig)?</div>
<p>Legend has it, in ancient agrarian China, a home (家) was where humans <em>and</em> their livestock sheltered under one roof, pigs were the most valuable livestock. So "a pig under a roof" = a prosperous, whole household.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> The 宀 (roof) = building/dwelling pattern: <strong>字</strong> (zì, character) = 宀 + 子, legend has it characters are "born" (taught) at home.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> Don't read it literally as "a home must have a pig", this is a picture of society 3000 years ago, not a dictionary definition.</p>
</div>

### 14. 土, tǔ, "earth"

Related to earth, places, and positions.

- 在 zài, to be at; in
- 坐 zuò, to sit; to ride (a vehicle)
- 块 kuài, measure word for money (yuan, spoken)

<div class="bedah"><strong>在</strong> (zài, at/to be at) = 土 (earth) + 才. The meaning "to be at" can't be derived from the components; memorize it whole. (In Chapter 5, 在 becomes a superstar verb: 我在学校 = I'm at school.)</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>坐</strong> (zuò, to sit) = 从 (two people) + 土 (earth) → "two people on the ground" = to sit → to ride (a vehicle).</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>块</strong> (kuài, money measure word) = 土 (earth) + 夬. Its original meaning is "lump/chunk" (of earth). Since money is counted per piece, 块 extended into the spoken measure word for yuan: 三块钱 (sān kuài qián) = three yuan.</div>

### 15. 饣 / 食, shí, "food"

The variant form of 食 on the left. Related to food.

- 米饭 mǐfàn, rice
- 饭店 fàndiàn, restaurant; hotel
- 饭馆 fànguǎn, eatery

<div class="bedah"><strong>饭</strong> (fàn, in <strong>米饭</strong> mǐfàn, rice) = 饣 (food) + 反 → rice/staple food.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>饭店</strong> (fàndiàn) = 饭 (food) + 店 (shop) → "food shop" = restaurant.</div>
<div class="bedah"><strong>饭馆</strong> (fànguǎn) = 饭 (food) + 馆 → eatery.</div>
