# Chapter 5 · 8–13: Modifiers & Adverbials

## 8. Demonstratives: 这 / 那 + measure word + noun

"This/that" can't attach directly to a noun. It must go through a measure word: **这/那 + [measure word] + noun**.

| 那 + measure word + noun | 那个人 |

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** 这书 / 那人 without a measure word sounds wrong. Always slip the measure word in the middle.

## 9. Measure words: 个 / 本 / 岁 / 块 / 些

Every noun is counted with its own "measure word". 个 (*gè*) is the all-purpose one, when in doubt, use 个. 本 for books, 岁 for age, 块 for money (spoken), 些 for "some".

## 12. Question words don't move position

Good news: Mandarin question words (什么, 谁, 哪, 哪儿, 多少, 几, 怎么, 怎么样) **stay in the position of the word being asked about**, they don't move to the front of the sentence like in Indonesian.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| question word stays in the asked position | 你吃什么？ |
| question word stays in the asked position | 你是谁？ |

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** the reflex is to move the question word to the front ("Apa kamu makan?"). In Mandarin, 什么 stays in object position: 你吃什么, not 什么你吃.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>Because there are no conjugations and word order is rigid (point 1), the cheapest way to form a question is to <strong>swap the unknown word for a question word in the same spot</strong>, like filling in a blank: 你吃___？ → 你吃什么？ <em>(nǐ chī shénme?)</em>. Indonesian instead moves its question word to the front (&quot;apa yang kamu makan?&quot;), which is why it feels odd at first. But Mandarin's logic is thriftier: the sentence structure doesn't change at all.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> all question words (谁, 哪, 哪儿, 多少, 几, 怎么, 怎么样) follow the same rule, none of them move.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> 什么你吃？ copies the Indonesian pattern, totally wrong in Mandarin.</p>
</div>

## 13. Time expressions: between subject & verb

Time expressions (今天, 明天, 昨天, 现在...) go **after the subject, before the verb**: [Subject] + [time] + [verb].

<div class="hz">我今天去学校。</div>
<div class="py">wǒ jīntiān qù xuéxiào.</div>
<div>I go to school today.</div>
</div>

<div class="contoh"><button class="audio-btn" data-audio="../audio/fba4a9658e39.mp3" aria-label="Listen to pronunciation" title="Listen"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="currentColor" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M8 5v14l11-7z"/></svg></button>

<div class="contoh"><button class="audio-btn" data-audio="../audio/54b7c60f1592.mp3" aria-label="Listen to pronunciation" title="Listen"><svg viewBox="0 0 24 24" fill="currentColor" aria-hidden="true"><path d="M8 5v14l11-7z"/></svg></button>
<div class="hz">我现在喝茶。</div>
<div class="py">wǒ xiànzài hē chá.</div>
<div>I'm drinking tea now.</div>
</div>

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + time expression + verb | 我今天去学校。 |
| Subject + time expression + verb | 他明天下午来。 |

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** the classic trap. "Saya pergi ke sekolah hari ini" translated word for word becomes 我去学校今天, wrong. Time goes before the verb.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>Mandarin puts the <strong>&quot;frame&quot; before the &quot;content&quot;</strong>: when it happens is said first, then the action, like setting the stage before the actors perform. 我今天去学校 <em>(wǒ jīntiān qù xuéxiào)</em> reads as &quot;[today] [I go to school]&quot;: the time is the backdrop, the action is the content. Indonesian is more flexible (&quot;saya pergi hari ini&quot; / &quot;hari ini saya pergi&quot;), but Mandarin picks one standard slot to avoid ambiguity.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 在 + place also goes before the verb (他在家学习, point 14), place is also a &quot;stage&quot; set up before the action.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> 我去学校今天 copies the Indonesian pattern, wrong.</p>
</div>
