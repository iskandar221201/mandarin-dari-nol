# Chapter 5: Basic Grammar

This chapter summarizes the 18 HSK 1 grammar patterns — the foundation for every sentence you'll build. Each pattern is covered in order: a short explanation, examples, and the trap Indonesian speakers fall into most often.

> **Reading note:** pinyin in this book is written in **base tone** (dictionary form). When speaking, apply the tone-change rules from Chapter 0 — e.g. 不 *bù* is read *bú* before a 4th tone (不是), 你好 *nǐ hǎo* is read *ní hǎo*.

Good news: Mandarin word order is exactly the same as Indonesian — **Subject – Verb – Object**. If you can make an Indonesian sentence, you can make a Mandarin one. Just swap the words.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + Verb + Object | 我学习汉语。 |
| Subject + Verb + Object | 他吃米饭。 |

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** the biggest temptation is putting the time expression at the end like in Indonesian ("Saya pergi ke sekolah *hari ini*"). In Mandarin, time expressions go before the verb — see point 13.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>Mandarin has no verb conjugation and no endings marking subject/object. So the only way to tell &quot;who does what to whom&quot; is a <strong>rigid word order</strong>: the doer first, the action in the middle, the target at the end. Indonesian happens to also be a &quot;plain&quot; language like this — which is why its S-V-O matches exactly and you can translate almost word for word.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> this rigid order explains many other patterns: question words don't move position (point 12), time expressions always sit in the same slot (point 13). If the order were random, the meaning would be random too.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> because the S-V-O matches, the biggest temptation is actually translating modifiers (time/place) word for word — even though Mandarin's modifier slots differ from Indonesian.</p>
</div>

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** 是 isn't used before adjectives. "I'm happy" isn't 我是高兴, it's 我很高兴 (see point 5). 是 also isn't used for location — "he's at home" is 他在家, not 他是在家.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| Statement + 吗？ | 你是学生吗？ |
| "No" answer: 不 + verb | 不喜欢。 |

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** don't combine 吗 with another question word. If the sentence already has 谁 / 什么 / 哪, 吗 isn't needed — 你是谁吗？ is wrong.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>吗 isn't a question word like &quot;whether&quot; — it's a <strong>sentence-mode marker</strong>, like the &quot;?&quot; mark in writing. The &quot;?&quot; always sits at the end of a sentence because it wraps the <em>entire</em> sentence; stuck in the middle, it only wraps half of it and the meaning gets weird. That's why 吗 is always at the end: 你是学生吗？ <em>(nǐ shì xuéshēng ma?)</em></p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 呢 (point 4) and 了 (point 18) are also always sentence-final — they're all sentence &quot;wrappers&quot;, not part of the content.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> since 吗 already flips the sentence into question mode, adding another question word (谁/什么) is doubled up and wrong.</p>
</div>

Adjectives can't be predicates on their own — they need a "bridge". The most neutral bridge is 很 (*hěn*). In this position 很 often does **not** mean "very", it's just a connector: 她很漂亮 = "she is pretty" (not "she is very pretty").

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** translating "she is pretty" raw as 她漂亮 — understandable, but the standard form uses 很. If you really mean "very/too", use point 6's pattern.

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>Mandarin adjectives can't stand alone as predicates — they need &quot;legs&quot; to hold them up. 很 is the most neutral leg: its job is only to <strong>connect the subject to its quality</strong>, not to strengthen the meaning. So 她很漂亮 <em>(tā hěn piàoliang)</em> is literally closer to &quot;she <em>is</em> pretty&quot; than &quot;she is <em>very</em> pretty&quot;. For a real &quot;very&quot; meaning, use the 太...了 pattern (point 6).</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 是 is the &quot;leg&quot; for identity (他是老师), 在 is the &quot;leg&quot; for location (他在家). Mandarin likes explicit bridges.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> translating "very pretty" as 很漂亮 — that's just plain "pretty".</p>
</div>

<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>The difference isn't just &quot;past vs not&quot;, it's <strong>judgment vs fact</strong>. 不 denies something that's a judgment, a habit, or a plan — something <em>not yet decided</em> or unverifiable (我不吃苹果 = &quot;I just don't eat apples&quot;). 没 denies a <strong>fact that already happened</strong> — something checkable (我没去商店 = &quot;in fact I didn't go&quot;). That's why the future uses 不 (hasn't happened = can't be checked) and the past uses 没.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 没有 (&quot;there isn't&quot;) = 没 + 有 — denying the fact of something's existence.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> 我昨天不去商店 — it already happened and is checkable, so it must use 没.</p>

## 18. 了: marker of changed state (introduction)

**Trap for Indonesian speakers:** don't treat 了 as the English "-ed" or as "already" that always means past. Its past/completion job only gets fully covered in HSK 2. For now remember: 了 = "this is the situation now".

<div class="kenapa">
<div class="kenapa-title">🧩 Why is the pattern like this?</div>
<p>了 isn't a past-tense marker — it's a <strong>change-of-state</strong> marker. 下雨了 <em>(xià yǔ le)</em> means &quot;the situation now is rain&quot;, implying &quot;it wasn't before&quot;. That's why 了 can also appear in future sentences (later in HSK 2): what matters is a <em>transition</em> from one state to another, not when it happens.</p>
<p><strong>Same pattern:</strong> 太...了 needs 了 as a &quot;closer&quot; (point 6) — the sentence feels complete because it asserts a new state.</p>
<p><strong>Watch out:</strong> equating 了 with the English "-ed" — even though 我昨天去商店 (without 了) is already correct for the past.</p>
</div>

4. Fill in the blank with the right measure word: 这___书是我的。
5. Fix this word order: 我去学校今天。
6. Pick the correct one for "I didn't go to the store yesterday": A. 我昨天不去商店 B. 我昨天没去商店
7. Translate to Mandarin: "There are many doctors at the hospital."
8. Arrange these scrambled words into a correct sentence: 太 / 热 / 了 / 今天
9. Arrange these scrambled words into a correct sentence: 我 / 也 / 喜欢 / 狗
10. Pick the correct one for "He studies at home": A. 他在家学习 B. 他是在家学习
11. Translate to Mandarin: "I have one book."
12. Fill in the blank with the right word (also): 她___喜欢茶。
13. Pick the WRONG one: A. 你吃什么？ B. 什么你吃？
14. Translate to Mandarin: "This apple is too big."
15. Arrange these scrambled words into a correct sentence: 看 / 电影 / 他 / 昨天

<details><summary>Answer key</summary>

1. 他是学生吗？(*tā shì xuéshēng ma?*) — "Is he a student?"
2. **B.** Adjectives need the bridge 很, not 是.
3. 她也喜欢茶。(*tā yě xǐhuan chá.*)
4. **本** — books are counted with 本: 这本书.
5. 我今天去学校。(*wǒ jīntiān qù xuéxiào.*) — time expressions go before the verb.
6. **B.** It already happened (yesterday) → use 没.
7. 医院里有很多医生。(*yīyuàn lǐ yǒu hěn duō yīshēng.*)
8. 今天太热了。(*jīntiān tài rè le.*)
9. 我也喜欢狗。(*wǒ yě xǐhuan gǒu.*) — "I also like dogs."
10. **A.** Locations use 在, without 是.
11. 我有一本书。(*wǒ yǒu yī běn shū.*)
12. **也** — 她也喜欢茶。(*tā yě xǐhuan chá.*)
13. **B** is wrong. Question words don't move position: 什么 stays in object position.
14. 这个苹果太大了。(*zhège píngguǒ tài dà le.*)
15. 他昨天看电影。(*tā zuótiān kàn diànyǐng.*) — "He watched a movie yesterday." Time expressions go before the verb.
