# Dissection Traps

*Dissection is a tool, not magic. Know its limits so you don't get trapped.*

The [Hanzi Dissection Method](/en/metode-bedah-hanzi) works on 80% of hanzi. This page covers the other 20%: when dissection fails, why it fails, and what to do then.

## 1. Cultural logic, not universal logic

好 (hǎo, good) = 女 (woman) + 子 (child). "Woman + child = good" made sense in ancient agrarian China: a whole, fertile family was seen as good. But this logic **can't be derived from pure common sense**. If you didn't live in that culture, there's no way to guess 好 from its components.

The lesson: dissection sometimes gives an *origin story*, not a *formula*. The story helps you remember, but don't force it into universal logic.

## 2. Sound hints often miss

Sound hints are "roughly", not guaranteed. Levels of missing:

- **Missed tone** (most common): 马 (mǎ) → 妈 (mā), 吗 (ma). Same base sound, different tone.
- **Big miss**: sometimes only the vowel or initial consonant matches; sometimes it drifted entirely over thousands of years of sound change.

The rule: use sound hints for *remembering*, not for *determining*. Always check the dictionary/audio for the exact sound.

## 3. Simplification destroys hints

Simplified hanzi sometimes cut the most informative component:

- **听** (tīng, to listen): the traditional **聽** uses 耳 (ear), which makes sense. The simplified form borrows 口 + 斤, which is confusing if dissected raw.
- **爱** (ài, to love): the traditional **愛** still has 心 (heart) at the bottom; the simplified form lost its heart component.

The lesson: if dissecting the simplified form makes no sense at all, peek at the traditional form; the pattern is often still intact there.

## 4. Meanings drift into idioms

Sometimes the components are clear but the meaning has jumped far:

- **东西** (dōngxi, thing/stuff) = 东 (east) + 西 (west). No way to guess "thing" from the components.
- **打电话** (dǎ diànhuà, to make a phone call) = "to hit the telephone", a leftover from rotary phones dialed by hand.

This isn't method failure; it's language evolving. Dissection explains the *origin*; usage explains the *current meaning*.

## 5. When to stop dissecting

Practical rules:

1. Dissect at most ~30 seconds per hanzi. If the pattern doesn't show up, **memorize it whole and move on**. Don't get stuck on one hanzi.
2. Dissection is a *guessing* and *remembering* aid, not a dictionary replacement. The order is always: **dissect first to guess, dictionary to confirm.**
3. Opaque hanzi (like 东西, 在, 哪) exist, and that's normal. 80% patterned is more than enough to multiply your learning speed.
