# The Hanzi Dissection Method

*Hanzi are understood, not memorized.*

## 1. The problem: you can't memorize 3,000 hanzi one by one

HSK 1 needs around 150 hanzi. HSK 6 needs around 2,600. If every hanzi is memorized as a random, unrelated picture, your brain gives up long before HSK 3.

The good news: hanzi form a **combinatorial system**, not 3,000 random pictures. The building blocks are only about 200 radicals plus a few hundred sound components. From that limited set, thousands of hanzi are "assembled".

Think of it like chemistry: nobody memorizes every molecule one by one. What you learn is the **periodic table** (the components) plus **how atoms combine** (the patterns). Hanzi dissection is the periodic table of Chinese script.

## 2. Anatomy: hanzi have positional structures

Before dissecting, learn the six positional structures of hanzi. Position tells you where to look for the radical (usually left, top, or outside) and where to look for the sound hint (usually right, bottom, or inside).

| Structure | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Left-right 左右 | ⿰ | 你 = 亻 + 尔 · 妈 = 女 + 马 |
| Top-bottom 上下 | ⿱ | 想 = 相 + 心 · 字 = 宀 + 子 |
| Full enclosure 全包围 | ⿴ | 国 = 囗 + 玉 |
| Half enclosure 半包围 | ⿵⿶⿷⿸⿹ | 床 · 庭 · 历 |
| Triple stack 品字 | ⿳ | 品 · 森 · 晶 |
| Single 独体 | — | 人 · 口 · 日 · 木 (can't be dissected further) |

The **left-right** structure is by far the most common (the majority of HSK 1 hanzi), so master that one first.

## 3. Two roles of components

Every hanzi component plays one of two roles:

- **Meaning hint** — usually the radical (部首). Examples: 氵 means "water", 扌 means "hand", 口 means "mouth". This component tells you the *category* of the hanzi's meaning.
- **Sound hint** — the phonetic component. Examples: 马 (mǎ) hints the sound is roughly "ma", 也 (yě) hints the sound is roughly "ye/ta".

Over 80% of hanzi are **形声字** (xíngshēngzì, "sound-meaning characters"): one component for meaning, one for sound. Once you can separate "which part gives meaning, which part gives sound", an unfamiliar hanzi becomes 50% easier to guess.

## 4. The four dissection steps

The technique is always the same, four steps:

1. **Split** the hanzi into its components (using the positional structures from section 2).
2. **Find the radical** → guess the meaning category (water? hand? mouth? woman?).
3. **Look at the remaining component** → guess the sound (how is this component read?).
4. **Combine + check a dictionary.** Dissection is a guessing and memorizing aid, not a dictionary replacement.

### Walkthrough 1: 想 (xiǎng, to want/to think)

1. Split (top-bottom): 相 + 心
2. Radical: 心 (heart) → category of feelings/thoughts. Ancient Chinese located thoughts and feelings in the heart, not the brain.
3. Sound: 相 (xiāng) → sounds roughly like "xiang".
4. Combine: "something heart-related that sounds like xiang" → 想 = to think/to want. Check the dictionary ✓.

### Walkthrough 2: 语 (yǔ, language — in 汉语 Hànyǔ)

1. Split (left-right): 讠 + 吾
2. Radical: 讠 (speech) → category of language/talking.
3. Sound: 吾 (wú) → sounds roughly like "wu".
4. Combine: "speech-related, sounds roughly like wu/yu" → 语 = language. Check the dictionary ✓.

Notice: in both examples the sound guess isn't 100% exact (xiāng → xiǎng differs in tone, wú → yǔ drifts). That's normal — sound hints are "roughly", and "roughly" is enough for remembering.
